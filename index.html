Applied nutrition data

Nutrition data infrastructure for Indian food.

We build the measurement layer that makes Indian dietary assessment work, starting with the portion units Indian kitchens actually use.

The measurement gap

Food composition databases store nutrient values per 100 grams. Indian households do not measure food in grams. They measure in katoris, rotis, ladles and tumblers. Almost every dietary tracking tool available in India bridges that gap by asking the user to guess, or by silently applying a Western default serving size.

The gap is not a rounding error. The same 150 ml katori holds very different weights depending on what is in it and how it was cooked.

Multiply those units across several thousand regional dishes and every cell has to be measured rather than estimated. That table does not exist as a public resource. Building and validating it is our current work.

What we build

Home-portion ontology

A validated mapping from household serving vessels to weights, resolved by food class and cooking method, across Indian regional cuisines.

Voice-first dietary logging

People describe a meal in their own language and in their own units. Speech and language handling for Indic languages, not a translated interface.

Correction as measurement

Every user correction to an estimated portion is a data point. Aggregated, it produces empirical portion distributions across regions.

How we work with data

Indian food composition data is the product of decades of publicly funded scientific work. We treat it accordingly.

  • We license, we do not scrape. Where a source requires written permission for commercial use, we obtain it before building on the data.
  • Attribution is permanent and visible. Source institutions are credited in the product itself, not only in a buried acknowledgements page.
  • We do not redistribute source datasets. Underlying tables are never republished as a standalone dataset.
  • Measurement flows back. Anonymised, aggregated portion data we generate is offered back to the research institutions whose work we build on, at no cost.

Our foundational references are the Indian Food Composition Tables 2017, published by ICMR–National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad, and the Indian Nutrient Databank, developed by Anuvaad Solutions. Neither institution is affiliated with Ariyah Labs, and neither has endorsed this work.

Current stage

Ariyah Labs is at the data foundation stage. We are assembling and validating the portion ontology before building consumer product, because the ontology is the part that determines whether anything built on top of it is accurate.

We are actively seeking data partnerships and licensing conversations with nutrition research institutions. If your organisation holds Indian food composition or dietary intake data, we would value a conversation.
